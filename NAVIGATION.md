# Wreck survey navigation

## What runs in ocean_web

The submarine tracks 17 preplanned 3D waypoints from a known launch pose. Modelica synthesizes four DVL beams, pressure, gyro and accelerometer measurements, a grayscale navigation camera, and a forward-imaging sonar fan with 65 bearings and 128 range bins. The estimator propagates position with DVL and inertial measurements, uses pressure for vertical position, and corrects horizontal position and yaw by registering sonar scans. Camera patches contribute yaw information and help verify revisits. Control uses the resulting measurements for the waypoint error, velocity, heading and attitude feedback. Simulated true state generates the sensor readings and provides a reference for evaluation.

In the integrated survey test, the submarine completed all 17 stations in 172.2 simulated seconds. The position estimate had 0.215 m RMS error and recorded 48 keyframes with seven loop closures. This is localization on a known route under simulated sensing conditions. The renderer draws Gaussian splats at the measured sonar returns and redraws their world positions when a loop closure updates the keyframes.

These metrics describe distinct test routes and reference measurements. The simulation’s RMS trajectory error and a field experiment’s marker-revisit error measure different quantities.

## Applying the evidence

For this wreck, a practical architecture is high-rate IMU propagation and a bounded local smoothing window. The window can fuse individual valid DVL beam velocities, pressure depth with a bias term, and relative pose constraints from sonar scan-to-submap registration. Visual reprojection factors can enter when feature coverage and geometric consistency support them. Sonar factors also need consistency tests because repeated bulkheads and specular returns can produce plausible false matches. Revisits should pass geometric verification before correcting the global pose graph. [SVIn+](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36454v1) · [AQUA-SLAM](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10938346) · [Compact 3D sonar mapping](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18991v1)

Piccard integrates returns over a 20° vertical aperture to form the 2D sonar image. The estimator receives dominant ranges across the fan. Its map places each return at the beam's central elevation. Overlapping views and correctly associated optical observations can narrow the resulting vertical uncertainty. A clearance map for control should represent occupied, free and unknown space, with confidence in each contact. A display splat encodes a return and its spread. It does not establish that a passage is clear. [Opti-acoustic volumetric mapping (ICRA 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14457v1)

NAS-GS and Z-Splat show how a denser scene model can be optimized from overlapping sonar images or from camera–sonar data. They use an image-formation model and multiple viewpoints. The browser’s sparse Gaussian overlay visualizes existing measurements at keyframes. [NAS-GS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06285) · [Z-Splat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04687)

Field readiness requires calibrated geometry and clocks, a repeatable sensor-log interface, realistic acoustic failure modes, and tests of degraded conditions. A useful progression starts with partial and lost DVL bottom lock, missing or biased visual measurements, pressure drift, and sonar dropout and multipath. Compare tracking error, loop-closure precision, clearance, completion and compute time with each sensor disabled in turn. Replay synchronized field recordings before using the estimator in a controlled-water vehicle trial.

## Optical navigation and Gaussian reconstruction for Piccard

Focused literature review, November 2026. The software currently tracks small
patches in a 36 × 24 synthetic grayscale camera at 5 Hz. The color image shown
to the pilot comes from a separate render. The estimator uses optical motion to
correct yaw and optical descriptors to help verify revisits. Its map stores up
to 48 leveled sonar returns per keyframe. A pilot can recognize a doorway in
the color view today, but the estimator does not yet triangulate its corners.

### Implementation and validation priorities

1. Feed the estimator timestamped, calibrated frames from the same camera and lens model the pilot uses. Keep an unannotated image before HUD composition. Track sparse landmarks across several keyframes and minimize the pixel error between their observed and predicted positions. Reject poorly distributed or ambiguous tracks. Keep IMU, pressure, DVL and sonar updates available through lighting changes.
2. Calibrate the camera and sonar together in water. Project each acoustic range–azimuth observation through its possible elevations into the camera. Accept a height only with a consistent visual association or supporting views. Represent the remaining elevation uncertainty explicitly. Sideways and vertically offset survey passes add parallax. [Opti-acoustic calibration and 3D reconstruction](https://people.csail.mit.edu/hpirsiav/papers/opti_acoustic_TIP09.pdf)
3. Use the estimated poses and selected RGB keyframes for an optional dense mapper. Seed Gaussians with triangulated landmarks or associated sonar ranges, refine with overlapping views and maintain an uncertainty for geometric use. Test moving-headlamp illumination, shadows and suspended particles. A cross-regime preprint found that the tested medium-aware methods could lose to conventional splatting under a camera-mounted lamp. [Controlled lighting study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25483)
4. Compare the current estimator, visual reprojection enabled, and visual plus sonar range factors on the same logs. Use the Tank dataset's calibrated camera, IMU, DVL and depth data for trajectory evaluation. Use the Shipwreck Sensing Dataset's real monocular, sonar and IMU recordings to check cross-modal associations on car and ship wrecks. Measure metric trajectory drift, relocalization failures and false closures, independent surface error where surveyed geometry exists, and p95 update latency. Track held-out image quality separately. Simulated wreck geometry belongs only in sensor generation and evaluation. [Tank dataset](https://doi.org/10.1177/02783649251364904) · [Shipwreck Sensing Dataset and calibration paper](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/3/1700)
